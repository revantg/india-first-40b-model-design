A0 · Budget

14T, derived twice.

Claim. Parameter count is given by the brief, so the only open question is how many tokens — and that number is the denominator for every figure in the mixture. It is not picked from a range. Two independent constraints squeeze it from opposite sides: compute parity sets a floor, unique-data supply sets a ceiling, and 14T is what survives both.

Constraint 1 — the floor: what parity actually costs

Training costs about 6 FLOPs per parameter per token: 2 forward, 4 backward. So C = 6ND. The parity reference is Gemma 3 27B on 14T tokens, which is published.

target     C = 6 × 27e9 × 14e12 = 2.27e24 FLOPs
ours 14T  C = 6 × 40e9 × 14e12 = 3.36e24 FLOPs  →  1.48× the target

Matching compute exactly needs only 9.4T at 40B, because we carry 29% more parameters than the 27B reference. That is the floor: below it, “as good as Gemma 4” is arithmetically unavailable. 14T buys 1.48× the target’s compute — real headroom, not a coin flip. This is also why the Chinchilla row fails: 40B on 0.8T is 1.92e23 FLOPs, 11.8× less than the target, and the paper’s own fitted law L = 1.69 + 406.4/N^0.34 + 410.7/D^0.28 puts it at loss 1.982 against the target’s 1.891.

Distillation is part of the selected recipe, not just a stick to beat the small budgets with. Stages A and B run with knowledge distillation from a larger teacher — top-k logits per token weighted by teacher probability, the mechanism the 27B reference itself used. It is what buys quality at 14T instead of requiring 20T, and it is why we can stay inside the epoch discipline rather than repeating data to reach a bigger headline number.

The weakest link, stated plainly: the 4.8T unique figure is assembled from published corpus sizes, not measured after running our own pipeline end to end. If the real filtered yield is 6T, 18T becomes defensible; if it is 3.5T, the budget drops to about 10T. Measuring it therefore gates the run — the same “ablate before you scale” discipline applied to the budget itself.

Unique stock ≈4.8T measured against published corpus sizes; 14T is 2.9 average passes, hard cap 4 on any sub-corpus — repeats are nearly free to ~4 epochs and near-worthless by ~16 (Muennighoff et al.). Effective public human text is ~300T (Epoch AI), so the shortfall is filled by licensed Indian sources, new languages and capped synthetic, not by more crawling.

How 14T is partitioned before any mixture is chosen

Decision. 40B dense with distillation, 14T tokens at 350 per parameter, being 1.48× the parity target’s compute and 2.9 epochs on a 4.8T unique pool, partitioned 12.67T scored / 1.12T protected / 210B reserved for the cooldown.
A1 · Data — what, how much, why

Four stages. A supply-capped ledger.

Claim. 14T pre-training tokens across four stages that sum exactly to 14T. The unit of data changes after pre-training, so each stage gets its own table. The single hardest constraint in the whole brief: verified native Indic text barely exists, so the Indic bucket is sized by supply and by the synthetic ceiling, not by ambition.

Code is 18% of Stage A and 17.6% of the whole run (1.94T here plus 0.52T in Stage B, of 14T) — stated as a run-wide figure, not a Stage-A figure dressed up as a cap. Code lifts non-code reasoning by up to +8.2% in the best published case, but an excessive share measurably hurts morphology and factual recall. Documents carry multiple tags, so a Tamil physics lesson satisfies both the Indic and STEM floor while being sampled once.

Total Indic-language exposure is larger than the bucket: 0.76T native + 0.05T real romanized + ~0.67T of the 1.12T always-on floor + 0.26T in Stage B + 0.05T of the anneal ≈ 1.79T, about 12.8% of the run, on top of 1.62T of India-context English. Verified reality check: Telugu falls from a 16.3B headline to 3.7B verified and Odia from 12.5B to 1.2B, against 2,847B English in CulturaX. Synthetic stays ≤25% of the corpus overall and ≤50% inside a scarce-language bucket, always grown from a verified seed, because a corpus that feeds on model output degrades.

Stage B Indic is 10%, not 25%: a top decile of the Stage A Indic pool is ~0.08T, so even 0.26T is roughly 3 passes of the very best material and has to be widened beyond the strict decile. Claiming 25% here would mean tens of epochs on a few billion tokens, contradicting the same epoch cap stated above. The anneal must be reserved before training starts: a small cooldown mix of exactly this shape moved grade-school math from 24.1% to 67.5% in OLMo 2. The floor exists because an English-trained selector systematically under-scored Brahmic text in V4; the published fix reserved 8% of every batch across 10 scripts, 66.9B tokens of guaranteed Indic inside that channel.

Post-training — volume falls, precision rises

StageVolumeComposition and why
SFT · instruction → response~1M pairs35% agentic and code (sandbox-verified trajectories, verified tool-calls) · 25% reasoning chains · 25% Indic authored natively, never translated · 15% India-domain and safety. A bad answer here is copied verbatim
Preference · chosen vs rejected~120K prompts × 4 responses ≈ 500K pairsBooked in pairs, which is the unit the published band uses, so this sits at the band ceiling rather than 3–5× above it. Indian annotator pool balanced across language, region, rural/urban, gender and community. No reward for verbosity; safety pairs stay human-anchored
RL with verifiable rewards~100K tasksGRPO against unit tests, symbolic math checks, tool-call schemas and ~5K sandboxed SWE / tool environments. Reward is the environment end-state, not a judge model

All three sit at the top of the published per-stage bands (SFT 100K–1M, preference 50–500K pairs, RL 10–100K prompts) rather than above them. Required volume falls roughly a millionfold from pre-training to RL while noise tolerance inverts — a mislabelled verifier teaches the wrong behaviour directly. Indian tools are in the sandbox (UPI, GST, IRCTC, DigiLocker, eCourts), and a language-consistency reward stops Indic reasoning from collapsing into English.

Decision. Stages summing to 14.0T, an Indic bucket capped at 0.76T by verified supply and the synthetic ceiling, the licensed Indian corpus booked as English where it actually is, and Indic post-training authored natively rather than translated.
A2 · India-first by construction

Every layer, not the RLHF layer.

Claim. “Views the world from an Indian perspective” must be produced by a mechanism at each layer, and each mechanism must produce a default that a probe can check.

LeverMechanismDefault it produces
Pre-training corpusIndian-authored English upweighted (press, textbooks, .in); India injections ×2–4: Constitution and BNS, Supreme and High Court judgments in bulk, Parliament debates, RBI / SEBI, NCERT / NPTEL, Census, UPI / ONDC docs; quality classifiers calibrated on Indian English so local idiom is not scored as low quality“The Constitution” resolves to India’s
Knowledge frameNCERT, BNS, RBI and Census treated as canonical for civics, law, finance and geography“Tax” resolves to GST and the IT Act
Structure over proseJudgments parsed as facts → issues → statutes → holding; dissents preserved; temporal citation graphs retainedLegal reasoning, not legal trivia
SFTNatively written Indian scenarios: UPI dispute, IRCTC booking, ration card, monsoon sowing, board exams₹, lakh / crore, Indian names and examples

InferenceLocale-default system promptDD-MM-YYYY, IST, Indian units

India-first is not government- or party-aligned. Contested questions are answered with factual attribution and constitutional pluralism, and the corpus retains multiple credible Indian perspectives rather than one.

Decision. Seven levers, each producing a default a probe can check. The evaluation row is what makes the claim falsifiable.
A3 · Cleaning for the objectives

Identify first. Filter last.

Claim. Most of the quality comes from what is removed, so each bucket gets its own pipeline. A single English-tuned gate would improve the majority language and quietly delete the minority ones.

BucketPipeline
All dataArticle-level extraction (drop navigation, cookie banners, ghost tags, HTML entities, U+FFFD, zero-width and control characters) → exact plus MinHash-LSH dedup at per-snapshot scope → Indian PII scrub including Aadhaar, PAN and UPI formats → 13-gram plus semantic decontamination against the whole evaluation suite, including translated variants → per-shard passport recording source, licence, language, natural or synthetic status and every transform
English webEducational-value classifier plus an AI-slop and content-farm detector
IndicTriple language-ID that rejects wrong-script documents · NFC normalisation preserving ZWJ / ZWNJ · byte-level MinHash on character 5-grams after canonicalising transliteration · per-language perplexity gates used as a soft filter so informal registers survive · natively built casteist and communal slur lexicons · romanized text kept and tagged separately from machine transliteration · machine-translation quality gate (COMET / LaBSE plus round-trip) on all synthetic
CodeLicence allowlist · secrets scrub · repository dedup · AST, lint and compile filters · issue and PR threads kept intact
Agentic tracesSandbox replay; keep successes and valid error-recovery only
Math / scienceLaTeX-preserving extraction; the answer-verifiable subset tagged for RL

Two India-specific rules generic pipelines get wrong

  • Preserve ZWJ / ZWNJ. Blind normalisation shatters conjuncts — the lesson from building the tokenizer in Session 2.
  • Deduplicate at byte level on character n-grams, after canonicalising transliteration. Word-level MinHash fails on agglutinative Dravidian morphology, and native and romanized copies are near-duplicates only once script is unified.

Dedup scope is an ablation, not an opinion

ChoiceTokens keptPublished outcome
Per-snapshot dedup~20TDefault. Matched the stronger baseline in FineWeb’s own ablation
Global dedup across all crawls~4TRemoves more and scores worse — it discards higher-quality older material

Every filter, threshold, dedup scope and synthetic ratio is tested on equal-compute 1B and 3B proxies with per-language reporting before it touches 14T. A change that raises the global average while dropping any protected language is rejected. Two classifier families are trained, not one: an English educational-value scorer, and native-labelled scorers per script family.

Decision. Language and script are identified before any quality score runs, protected Indic has retention floors, and every cleaning change must survive a proxy ablation.
A4 · Testing against the objectives

Gates set against live competitors.

Claim. A gate that a smaller published model already clears is not a gate. Every bar below is pinned to a current competitor or to a versioned harness, and none of them can be satisfied by a strong English score alone.

ObjectiveSuite → bar
General parityMMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, IFEval, Arena-Hard → within 2–3 points of Gemma 4 31B dense, on identical prompts, tools and token budgets
CodingSWE-bench Verified ≥ 65% with the scaffold and harness pinned — parity with the best published sub-30B coding models, which already reach 68–70%. A 40% bar would put us ~28 points behind a 24B open model. Plus LiveCodeBench (date-gated) and RepoBench; HumanEval is a smoke test only
AgenticBFCL v3 ≥ 78% (open SOTA is ~77.8%; v4 reported separately because it is materially harder), τ-bench, terminal-bench, an OSWorld subset → success and steps and cost; plus private held-out environments
IndicMILU (beat Gemma 4 31B), IndicGenBench, IN22 chrF++, romanization-robustness gap < 5%, Hinglish QA — reported per language, never averaged
India-firstPolity, schemes and GST factuality (UPSC-style), default-perspective probes, refusal-balance parity across religion / caste / region, quarterly Indian-rater evaluation
SafetyScored in-language. Published Indic work finds only ~13% cross-language agreement on safety decisions, so an English-only pass is a false pass
ContinuousPer-domain and per-language loss dashboards, tokenizer-fertility regression in CI, decontamination audit before any reported number

The firewall — three tiers, one rule

TierMay it train?Rule
Training pools (four stages, SFT, preference, RL)YesDecontaminated against every test set with exact, fuzzy, semantic and cross-lingual matching
Benchmark train splitsYesAllowed inside the always-on floor to teach answer format. The matching test split never enters a batch
Golden proxy — test and validation materialNeverImmutable, stored where training jobs have no read access. Used only to steer the selector and score the model

A leaked item does not merely inflate one score; it permanently invalidates that item as a test. Translated leakage counts — an English benchmark item appearing in Hindi is still contamination.

Decision. Release gates on every objective simultaneously, each pinned to a live competitor or a versioned harness, with per-language reporting and a firewall enforced at ingestion.
A5 · Fertility targets → tokenizer size

Per language, against measured numbers.

Claim. Fertility is tokens per unit of meaning; under a fixed budget a high-fertility language silently receives fewer documents. Targets are set per language against a published 200K Indic tokenizer, not as comfortable groupings. The generic tax it removes is 1.3–7.8× depending on language — about 1.4× for Hindi and Urdu, 7.8× for Odia — so the dispersion, not a headline floor, is the problem being solved.

Cost equity, stated honestly. Against English at 1.30, the ≤1.4× principle holds for 8 of the 12 scheduled languages (Hindi 0.96×, Punjabi 1.08×, Urdu 1.12×, Marathi 1.23×, Odia 1.27×, Bengali 1.31×, Gujarati 1.35×, Assamese 1.38×). The four Dravidian languages sit at 1.42× (Telugu), 1.58× (Tamil), 1.62× (Kannada) and 1.69× (Malayalam). That gap is a stated limitation, not a rounding error: closing it needs morphology-aware segmentation, not more vocabulary.

Construction: byte-level BPE, akshara-aware pre-tokenization per Brahmic script, ZWJ / ZWNJ preserved, digits split, superword merges evaluated as a second stage. The tokenizer’s own training mix ≈ 35% English and code, 45% Indic (fertility-driven upsample), 10% math, 10% other. Tied embeddings cost 196,608 × 6,144 = 1.21B parameters, 3.02% of 40B. Candidates at 160K, 196,608, 224K and 256K are compared on equal-FLOP 1B proxies; 196,608 ships only if per-language fertility, bits-per-byte, throughput and HumanEval / MBPP all hold. Per-language fertility is tracked in CI on every release, using the harness built in Session 2.

Decision. 196,608 byte-fallback BPE — 94K English/code/math, 76K Dravidian-weighted Indic across 10 scripts, 12K romanized, 10K other and reserved. Rejected 262K because measured fertility does not improve, not because it is unaffordable.
